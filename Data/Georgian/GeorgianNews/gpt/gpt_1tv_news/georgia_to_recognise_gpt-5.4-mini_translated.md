By decision of the government, legislative amendments on the recognition of diplomas issued before 2010 will be prepared  
10:44, 10.04.2026

According to the decision of the Government of Georgia, legislative amendments will be submitted to Parliament, on the basis of which the problems related to the recognition of diplomas issued before 2010 will be resolved for thousands of citizens, said Givi Mikanadze, Minister of Education, Science and Youth of Georgia.

As the minister noted, under the amendment, recognition of 4-year full-time and 5-year part-time studies will be treated equally.

According to him, the change will affect students who were enrolled at a university in a recognized mode and completed their studies in an unrecognized mode, as well as citizens who were enrolled in universities by educational institutions before obtaining a license.

“We had such a situation — our citizens completed exactly the same number of subjects in a 4-year program and received the same qualification as in a 5-year part-time program. Under the legislation in force until now, the 5-year program was equated with a master’s degree, while the 4-year program was awarded a bachelor’s degree. On the basis of the amendments, those who completed a 4-year program will receive a diploma equivalent to a master’s degree, which will significantly facilitate citizens’ employment in the field they desire.

Before 2010, before the unified system was established, there was no registry in the education sector that would ensure students were informed. There were cases when certain universities that did not have accreditation did not tell applicants about this and enrolled them anyway. Later, when they issued diplomas, graduates already faced problems with the recognition of this document.

In addition, a number of higher educational institutions had authorization for a certain period and then lost it during the study period. Students were not informed about this either, and later people who graduated from these universities had problems.

The proposed legislative amendments will finally put an end to the long and problematic processes our citizens go through to have their diplomas recognized — they go to court, challenge the processes, which takes time and effort. All of this will be consigned to history once and for all, and the diplomas of thousands of our citizens will be recognized,” said Givi Mikanadze.
