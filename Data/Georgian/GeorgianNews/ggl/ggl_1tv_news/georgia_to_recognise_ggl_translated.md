By decision of the government, legislative amendments on the recognition of diplomas issued before 2010 will be prepared
10:44, 10.04.2026

By decision of the government of Georgia, legislative amendments will be submitted to the parliament, on the basis of which thousands of citizens will be able to solve problems related to the recognition of diplomas issued before 2010, - said the Minister of Education, Science and Youth of Georgia, Givi Mikanadze.

As the minister noted, according to the amendment, 4-year full-time and 5-year part-time studies will be recognized equally.

According to him, the amendment will apply to students who enrolled in a university in a recognized regime and completed their studies in an unrecognized regime, as well as to citizens who were enrolled in a university before educational institutions obtained a license.

“We had such a situation – our citizens took exactly the same number of subjects in a 4-year program and received the same qualification as in a 5-year part-time program. According to the existing legislation, a 5-year program was equivalent to a master’s degree, and a 4-year program was awarded a bachelor’s degree. Based on the changes, those who completed a 4-year program will receive a diploma equivalent to a master’s degree, which will significantly facilitate the employment of citizens in their desired direction.

Until 2010, before a unified system was established, there was no register in the education sector that would ensure the information of students. There were cases when specific universities that had not been accredited did not inform applicants about this and enrolled them as they were. Later, when they issued a diploma, graduates were faced with the recognition of this document Problems.

In addition, a number of higher educational institutions had authorization for a certain period and then it was terminated during the study period. Students were not informed about this, and subsequently these university graduates had problems.

The presented legislative amendments will finally put an end to the long, problematic processes that our citizens go through to recognize their diplomas – they go to court, appeal the processes, which are associated with time and effort. All this will once and for all be history, and the diplomas of thousands of our citizens will be recognized,” said Givi Mikanadze.
